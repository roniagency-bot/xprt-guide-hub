Medicare, in plain language

The parts, and what they do.

  • Part A — HospitalHelps cover hospital stays, skilled nursing care after a hospital stay, hospice and some home health care.
  • Part B — MedicalHelps cover doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services and medical equipment. Most people pay a monthly premium for Part B.
  • Part C — Medicare AdvantagePrivate plans approved by Medicare that bundle Part A and Part B, and often Part D. They usually use a network of doctors, and plans differ by area.
  • Part D — Prescription drugsHelps pay for prescription medicines. Each plan has its own list of covered drugs, so your medicines matter when comparing.
  • Medigap — Medicare SupplementPrivate insurance that helps with some costs Original Medicare leaves to you, such as deductibles and coinsurance. It works with Parts A and B, not with Medicare Advantage.

When can you enroll or make changes?

  • Turning 65: a 7-month window — the 3 months before your birthday month, your birthday month, and the 3 months after.
  • −3 −2 −1 65 +1 +2 +3
    3 months before Your birthday month 3 months after
  • Every fall (Oct 15 – Dec 7): the Annual Enrollment Period, when you can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan for the next year.
  • Jan 1 – Mar 31: if you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can make one change during this period.
  • Life changes: moving, losing employer coverage and some other situations can open a special window.
